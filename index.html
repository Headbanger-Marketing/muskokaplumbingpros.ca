Plan the request

Arranging plumbing work when the owner cannot be on site

When the person who approves plumbing work cannot be at the property, a visit can stall or proceed on assumptions. For your Muskoka property, name who will give the provider access, what that person may approve and how photos and findings will reach you. If nothing is settled about contact times or limits, say so, because a written boundary on what may proceed without further approval is easier to agree before anyone attends.

  • Who gives access

    Name who will let the provider in and what that person may approve; leave keys and codes out of the request.

  • How you hear back

    Say how photos and findings should reach you, when you can reply, and whether a message to someone else can stand in.

  • Limit without approval

    Ask for a written limit on what may proceed before the provider must stop and check with you.

Before you send

Photos That Help Before the First Call

A few clear photos help a provider understand the job before the first call. Have them ready for the follow-up.

  • Water heater: the label with the model and capacity, the vent connection at the top of the tank, and any rental sticker or agreement.
  • Blocked drain: the fixtures that drain slowly or back up, whether it's only the lowest level, and the floor drain or cleanout if it's accessible.
  • Leak: the stain or damp patch, the water meter dial with every tap closed, and any recent renovation nearby.
  • Sump pump: the open pit and the pump inside it, the discharge pipe where it exits, and any backup pump or alarm.
  • Older supply pipe: the pipe beside the water meter. Copper looks orange-brown, galvanized steel is grey and threaded, and lead is dull grey and turns shiny silver where you scratch it with a key.
FAQ

Questions About Your Muskoka Request

Can someone else give access to my Muskoka property for a plumbing visit?

Yes, if you name that person in your request. Say who the person is to you, such as a relative or property manager, and what they may approve. Leave keys, door codes and alarm details out of the form. Practical arrangements are settled with the provider once the provider is identified and the scope is agreed.

What should a Muskoka plumbing request say about contact windows?

Give the hours when you or your named contact can reply, and say which of you answers first. Add how photos and findings should be sent, such as by message or email, and what should happen if there is no reply. A written note of those windows helps the provider avoid proceeding on assumptions during the visit.

If I send a request while away from my Muskoka property, is a plumbing visit already arranged?

No. Coverage, availability and pricing all remain unconfirmed when the request arrives. Our enquiry team reviews it first, and a provider must then confirm the address, the type of work, qualifications, written scope, pricing and scheduling before anything is agreed. Until that happens nothing has been arranged, whether or not you can be on site.

How can I limit what proceeds without my further approval?

Ask the provider to set out, in the written scope, which tasks may go ahead during the visit and which must pause for your answer. Useful pause points include opening a finished surface, replacing a major part and adding any item outside the agreed work. Agree the way of reaching you before the visit, not during it.

Should I send one Muskoka request for several plumbing jobs while away?

Send one request when the jobs are linked, such as a stain below a bathroom and a slow drain that serves it, and list each symptom separately. Unrelated items, such as a softener question and a tap replacement, are easier to approve one at a time, because each may need a different person to say yes.

What happens to the details I send about my Muskoka property?

Our enquiry team reviews the request and checks whether it describes the work, the access arrangement and who may approve. The details may then be passed to a provider, whose identity is confirmed to you before any work is agreed. Review does not produce a visit, a price or a promise of coverage; those are agreed with the provider afterwards.
